Harvard Business Review
The Cognitive Labor Divide: Why Some Jobs Thrive Under AI
Anna Chen · 12 min read · Saved Jun 18
The arrival of capable AI hasn't created a uniform shift in the labor market. Instead, it has sharpened a divide that was already forming: between workers who use their minds to frame and evaluate problems and those who primarily execute against defined tasks.
This distinction matters more than job title, industry, or even education level. A junior consultant who excels at structuring ambiguous situations may be safer than a senior analyst whose value is rooted in pattern-matching at high speed across structured data — precisely the thing large language models do well.
Your Note
This maps to the "taste vs. execution" framework — AI does execution well. The question is whether I'm building taste.
The implication for professionals isn't to abandon technical skills. It's to understand which parts of your work involve genuine judgment — where the output depends on your mental model of what good looks like…
